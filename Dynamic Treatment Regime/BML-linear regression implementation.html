BML- linear regression implementation

BML algorithms (Murray et al., 2018)

Stage 2 regression

Sampling distribution: \[Y_2(A_1, A_2) \mid H_2, A_2 ; \boldsymbol{\theta}_2 \sim f_2(y_2 \mid h_2, a_2; \boldsymbol{\theta}_2) \tag{1}\]

Prior for \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_2\): \[\boldsymbol{\theta}_2 \sim p_{2,0}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_2) \tag{2}\]

\(f_2\) can be a normal distribution with a parametric mean of for simplicity (standard Bayesian regression) or a normal model with nonparametric mean, e.g. BART, to better capture interaction effects.

e.g. 1. Standard Bayesian regression

We consider a set of parameters \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_2 := (\boldsymbol{\beta}_2, \sigma_2^2)\).

Let \(\boldsymbol{X}_2\) be a design matrix; \[ \boldsymbol{X}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & o_{11} & a_{11} & o_{11} \cdot a_{11} & o_{21} & a_{21} & a_{21} \cdot o_{11} & a_{21} \cdot a_{11} & a_{21} \cdot o_{21} \\ 1 & o_{12} & a_{12} & o_{12} \cdot a_{12} & o_{22} & a_{22} & a_{22} \cdot o_{12} & a_{22} \cdot a_{12} & a_{22} \cdot o_{22} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & o_{1i} & a_{1i} & o_{1i} \cdot a_{1i} & o_{2i} & a_{2i} & a_{2i} \cdot o_{1i} & a_{2i} \cdot a_{1i} & a_{2i} \cdot o_{2i} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & o_{1n} & a_{1n} & o_{1n} \cdot a_{1n} & o_{2n} & a_{2n} & a_{2n} \cdot o_{1n} & a_{2n} \cdot a_{1n} & a_{2n} \cdot o_{2n} \\ \end{pmatrix} \]

The first 5 columns are main effects of \(h_{2i}\) and the last 4 columns are the interaction terms with \(a_{2i}\), the main interests. Let \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2\) be the vector of regression coefficients. \[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} \beta_{20} & \cdots & \beta_{24} , \; \beta_{25} & \cdots & \beta_{28} \end{pmatrix}^T \]

Following equation (1), stage two response of an individual \(i\), \(y_{2i}\), follows a normal distribution; \[y_{2i} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2^{T} \boldsymbol{x}_{2i} , \sigma_{2}^2) \; \; \; i = 1,2,\dots,n\] where \(\boldsymbol{x}_{2i} := \begin{pmatrix} 1 & o_{1i} & a_{1i} & o_{1i} \cdot a_{1i} & o_{2i} & a_{2i} & a_{2i} \cdot o_{1i} & a_{2i} \cdot a_{1i} & a_{2i} \cdot o_{2i}\end{pmatrix}^T\) or equivalently, \[ \boldsymbol{y}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} y_{21} \\ y_{22} \\ \vdots \\ y_{2i} \\ \vdots \\ y_{2n} \end{pmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}_n (\boldsymbol{X}_2 \boldsymbol{\beta}_2, \sigma^2_2 \boldsymbol{I}_n) \]

For \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_2 \sim p_{2,0}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_2)\), we assume Zellner’s g-prior to achieve the invariance of regressors. Set \(g = n\) to approximate prior knowledge corresponding to one sample. (Hoff, 2009) \[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_2 \mid \sigma_2^2 \sim \mathcal{N}_9 (\boldsymbol{0}, n \sigma_2^2(\boldsymbol{X}_2^T \boldsymbol{X}_2)^{-1}) \] Set \(v_{20} = 1\) and \(\sigma_{20}^2 = \hat{\sigma}^2_{2,ols}\) \[ \sigma_2^2 \sim \text{inverse-Gamma} \left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2_{2,ols}}{2}\right) \]

Posterior of \(\sigma_2^2\): \[ \sigma_2^2 \mid \boldsymbol{y}_2, \boldsymbol{X}_2 \sim \text{inverse-Gamma} \left(\frac{n+1}{2}, \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2_{2,ols} + SSR_g}{2}\right) \] where \(SSR_g\) is given by \[ SSR_g = \boldsymbol{y_2}^T \;(\boldsymbol{I} - \frac{n}{n+1}\boldsymbol{X_2}(\boldsymbol{X_2}^T\boldsymbol{X_2})^{-1}\boldsymbol{X_2}^T)\boldsymbol{y_2} \]

Posterior of \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2\): \[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_2 \mid \boldsymbol{y}_2, \boldsymbol{X}_2, \sigma_2^2 \sim \mathcal{N}_9 \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{2,ols}, \frac{n}{n+1} \sigma_2^2(\boldsymbol{X}_2^T \boldsymbol{X}_2)^{-1}\right) \]

Note that BML requires full parametric modeling of the sampling variance \(\sigma^2\), compared to the semi-parametric form of Q-learning, which does not model the variance.

2 Stage 1 regression

The posterior of stage 1 parameters is given by the prior of stage 1 multiplied by the Likelihood given a fixed \(d_2^{opt}\) averaged over the stage 2 posterior.

\[ p_{1,n}(\boldsymbol{\theta_1}\mid d_2^{opt};D_n) = p_{1,0}(\boldsymbol{\theta_1}) \times \int_{\Theta_2} \; \{\text{Likelihood given a fixed} \; d_2^{opt} \}\;p_{2,n}(\boldsymbol{\theta_2}\mid D_n) d\boldsymbol{\theta_2} \tag{11} \]

Where, the likelihood given a fixed \(d_2^{opt}\), is given by multiplying likelihood of the observed values and counterfactual values averaged over the posterior predictive distribution of the counterfactual value!

\[ (\text{Likelihood given a fixed}\;d_2^{opt}) = \prod_{i:a_{2,i} = d_2^{opt}(h_{2,i};\boldsymbol{\theta_2})} f_1(y_{1,i} + y_{2,i} \mid h_1, a_1;\boldsymbol{\theta_1})\} \; \times \; \int_{Y_2} \{\prod_{i:a_{2,i} \neq d_2^{opt}(h_{2,i};\boldsymbol{\theta_2})}\; f_1(y_{1,i} + y_{2,i}^{mis}\mid h_{1,i},a_{1,i};\boldsymbol{\theta_1})\}\;f_{2,n} (\boldsymbol{y_{2}^{mis}}\mid D_n) d\boldsymbol{y_{2}^{mis}} \]

The goal of stage 1 regression is to derive the posterior \(p_{1,n}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_1 \mid d_2^{opt};\boldsymbol{D_n})\) according to (11). Sampling from (11) is not feasible with standard methods and we use the following BIG sampler instead.

BIG (Backward Induction Gibbs sampler) for deriving (11)

For \(s = 1,2,\cdots,S\):

Step 1. Sample stage 2 parameter from the stage 2 posterior.

\[\boldsymbol{\theta_2^{(s)}} \sim p_{2.n}(\boldsymbol{\theta_2}\mid \boldsymbol{D_n} )\] and determine the optimal decision \(a_{2,i}^{opt,(s)} = d_2^{opt}(h_{2,i};\boldsymbol{\theta_2^{(s)}})\) for \(i = 1,2,\cdots,n\) based on \(\boldsymbol{\theta_2^{(s)}}\).

Step 2.

Sample \(y_{2,i}^{mis,(s)} := y_{2,i}(a_{1,i}, a_{2,i}^{opt,(s)})\) for \(i = 1,2,\cdots,n\) from the stage 2 posterior predictive distribution.


\[ y_{2,i}^{mis,(s)} \sim f_{2,n}(y_2 \mid \boldsymbol{D_n}) \; \; \; \; \; i = 1,2,\cdots,n \]

\[ y_{2,i}^{opt,(s)} = \begin{cases} y_{2,i}^{mis,(s)} & \text{if}\; a_{2,i} \neq a_{2,i}^{opt,(s)} \\ y_{2,i} & \text{if}\; a_{2,i} = a_{2,i}^{opt,(s)} \end{cases} \quad \text{for all } i = 1,2,\cdots,n \]

For instance, the posterior predictive distribution would be multivariate t for linear regression. This can be verified by first integrating out \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) and then \(\sigma^2\).

\[ \tilde{\boldsymbol{y}} \mid \tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}, \boldsymbol{y}, \boldsymbol{X} \sim t_{\nu_0+n}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{ols},\ \frac{\nu_0\sigma_0^2 + SSR_g}{\nu_0+n}\left(\boldsymbol{I} + \frac{n}{n+1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{X}'\boldsymbol{X})^{-1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}'\right)\right) \]

Step 3.

Now generate a stage 1 dataset \(\boldsymbol{D_n^{opt,(s)}}\) using \(\{y_{2,i}^{opt,(s)}\}_{i = 1}^n\): \[\boldsymbol{D_n^{opt,(s)}} = \{h_{1,i},a_{1,i}, y_{1,i}, y_{2,i}^{opt,(s)} \}_{i = 1}^n\] And use this \(\boldsymbol{D_n^{opt,(s)}}\) to derive stage 1 posterior \(p_{1,n}(\boldsymbol{\theta_1} \mid \boldsymbol{D_n^{opt,(s)}})\) and sample one value from the posterior. \[ \boldsymbol{\theta_1^{(s)}} \sim p_{1,n}(\boldsymbol{\theta_1} \mid \boldsymbol{D_n^{opt,(s)}}) \]

Repeat Step 1 ~ Step 3 \(S\) times to generate: \[\text{Stage 1 Posterior Sample:} \; \{\boldsymbol{\theta_1^{(1)}},\boldsymbol{\theta_1^{(2)}}, \cdots, \boldsymbol{\theta_1^{(S)}}\}\] \[\text{Stage 2 Posterior Sample:} \; \{\boldsymbol{\theta_2^{(1)}},\boldsymbol{\theta_2^{(2)}}, \cdots, \boldsymbol{\theta_2^{(S)}}\}\]

e.g. 1. Standard Bayesian regression

Sampling distribution: \[Y_1(A_1, d_2^{opt}) \mid H_1, A_1 ; \boldsymbol{\theta}_1 \sim f_1(y_1 \mid h_1, a_1; \boldsymbol{\theta}_1) \tag{6}\]

\[ \boldsymbol{\theta}_1 \sim p_{1,0}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_1) \] We consider a set of parameters \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_1 := (\boldsymbol{\beta}_1, \sigma_1^2)\).

\(\boldsymbol{X}_1\) be a design matrix; \[ \boldsymbol{X}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & o_{11} & a_{11} & o_{11} \cdot a_{11} \\ 1 & o_{12} & a_{12} & o_{12} \cdot a_{12} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & o_{1i} & a_{1i} & o_{1i} \cdot a_{1i} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & o_{1n} & a_{1n} & o_{1n} \cdot a_{1n} \\ \end{pmatrix} \]

The first 2 columns are main effects of \(h_{1i}\) and the last 2 columns are the interaction terms with \(a_{1i}\), the main interests. Let \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1\) be the vector of regression coefficients. \[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} \beta_{10},\beta_{11},\beta_{12} , \beta_{13} \end{pmatrix}^T \]

Sampling distribution: \[ \boldsymbol{y}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} y_{11} \\ y_{12} \\ \vdots \\ y_{1i} \\ \vdots \\ y_{1n} \end{pmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}_n (\boldsymbol{X}_1 \boldsymbol{\beta}_1, \sigma^2_1 \boldsymbol{I}_n) \]

Priors: Use Zellner’s g-prior with \(g = n\). \[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_1 \mid \sigma_1^2 \sim \mathcal{N}_9 (\boldsymbol{0}, n \sigma_1^2(\boldsymbol{X}_1^T \boldsymbol{X}_1)^{-1}) \] Set \(v_{10} = 1\) and \(\sigma_{10}^2 = \hat{\sigma}^2_{1,ols}\) \[ \sigma_1^2 \sim \text{inverse-Gamma} \left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2_{1,ols}}{2}\right) \]

Posterior of \(\sigma_1^2\): \[ \sigma_1^2 \mid \boldsymbol{y}_1, \boldsymbol{X}_1 \sim \text{inverse-Gamma} \left(\frac{n+1}{2}, \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2_{1,ols} + SSR_{g}}{2}\right) \] where \(SSR_{g}\) is given by \[ SSR_g = \boldsymbol{y_1}^T \;(\boldsymbol{I} - \frac{n}{n+1}\boldsymbol{X_1}(\boldsymbol{X_1}^T\boldsymbol{X_1})^{-1}\boldsymbol{X_1}^T)\boldsymbol{y_1} \]

Posterior of \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1\): \[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_1 \mid \boldsymbol{y}_1, \boldsymbol{X}_1, \sigma_1^2 \sim \mathcal{N}_9 \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1,ols}, \frac{n}{n+1} \sigma_1^2(\boldsymbol{X}_1^T \boldsymbol{X}_1)^{-1}\right) \]

BML- linear regression on R

# Based on simulation 1, setting 6, of Murray et.al. 2018
library(MASS)
library(mvtnorm)
# true parameters; first half for main effect and second half for treatment effects
beta1 = c(0.644, 0.006, -0.369, 0.019) #stage 1 Q-function 
beta2 = c(0.00, 0.00, -0.50, 0.00, 0.00, 0.25, 0.00, 0.50, 0.50) # stage 2 Q-function
delta = c(0.1, 0.1) # o2 outcome model p(o2 | o1, a1) 

# sample 
set.seed(67);n = 200 # {-1,1} coding for covariates and assignment
o1 = 2* rbinom(n, 1, 0.5) -1 ; a1 = 2* rbinom(n, 1, 0.5) -1 
o2 = 2* rbinom(n, 1, 1/(1 + exp(-cbind(o1, a1) %*% delta))) -1 
a2 = 2 * rbinom(n, 1, 0.5) -1
y = rnorm(n, cbind(1, o1, a1, o1*a1,o2, a2,a2*o1,a2*a1, a2*o2 ) %*% beta2, 1)

my_dataset = list(data = cbind(o1, a1, o2, a2,y), beta1 = beta1, beta2 = beta2, delta = delta)

# check
View(my_dataset$data)
cor(my_dataset$data[,'a2'], my_dataset$data[,'y'])
[1] 0.3019777
boxplot(my_dataset$data[,'y'] ~my_dataset$data[,'a2'], 
        xlab = 'a2', 
        ylab = 'final payoff') 
# BIG sampler with g prior-------------------------------------------------------------
p1 = 4; p2 = 9 #dims of the parameters 
S = 2000
BETA1  = matrix(NA, nrow = S, ncol = p1)
SIGMA1 = rep(NA, S)
BETA2  = matrix(NA, nrow = S, ncol = p2)
SIGMA2 = rep(NA, S)
X1 = model.matrix(~ o1 + a1 + o1*a1)
X2 = model.matrix(~ o1 + a1 + o1*a1 + o2 + a2*(1 + o1 + a1 + o2))
#use model.matrix when constructing design matrices! 
treat_cols2 = grep('a2', colnames(X2)) # store the col names of treatment effects
treat_cols1 = grep('a1', colnames(X1))
main_cols2 = grep('a2', colnames(X2), invert = TRUE)
main_cols1 = grep('a1', colnames(X1), invert = TRUE)
fit2 = lm(y ~ o1 + a1 + o1:a1 + o2 + a2 + a2:o1 + a2:a1 + a2:o2)
Beta_2_ols  = coef(fit2)
sigma2_2_ols = summary(fit2)$sigma^2
SSRg_2 = as.numeric(t(y) %*% (diag(n) - n/(n+1) * (X2 %*% solve(t(X2)%*%X2)) 
                              %*% t(X2)) %*%y)

for(s in 1:S){
  #1. sample THETA.2 from stage 2 posterior
  sigma2_2 = 1/rgamma(1,shape = (n+1)/2,
                      rate = (sigma2_2_ols + SSRg_2)/2) 
  Beta_2 = mvrnorm(1, mu = n/(n+1) * Beta_2_ols, 
                   Sigma = n/(n+1) * sigma2_2 * solve(t(X2)%*%X2))
  BETA2[s, ] = Beta_2
  SIGMA2[s] = sigma2_2
  
  interaction = cbind(1, o1, a1, o2) %*% 
    Beta_2[treat_cols2]
  a2_opt = ifelse(interaction >= 0, 1, -1)
  
  #2. Step2: generate a counterfactual dataset 
  X2_new =  cbind(1,o1,a1,o1*a1,o2,
                  a2_opt, a2_opt*o1, a2_opt*a1, a2_opt*o2)
  y_mis = rmvt(1, df = n+1, delta = (n)/(n+1) *X2_new %*% Beta_2_ols, 
               sigma = (sigma2_2_ols + SSRg_2)/(n+1)*
                 (diag(n) + X2_new %*% solve(t(X2)%*%X2) %*% t(X2_new)),
               type = 'shifted'
  )
  y_counterfactual = y 
  for(i in 1:n){
    if(a2[i] != a2_opt[i]){
      y_counterfactual[i] = y_mis[i]
    }
  }
  
  #3 Step3: sample from stage 1 posterior using y2_counterfactual
  fit1 = lm(y_counterfactual ~ o1 + a1 + o1:a1)
  Beta_1_ols  = coef(fit1)
  sigma2_1_ols = summary(fit1)$sigma^2
  SSRg_1 = t(y_counterfactual) %*% 
    (diag(n) - n/(n+1) * (X1 %*% solve(t(X1)%*%X1)) %*% t(X1)) %*%
    y_counterfactual
  
  sigma2_1 = 1/rgamma(1,shape = (n+1)/2,
                      rate = (sigma2_1_ols + SSRg_1)/2) 
  
  Beta_1 = mvrnorm(1, mu = n/(n+1) * Beta_1_ols, 
                   Sigma = n/(n+1) * sigma2_1 * solve(t(X1)%*%X1))
  BETA1[s, ] = Beta_1
  SIGMA1[s] = sigma2_1
}


BETA2_EAP = colMeans(BETA2);BETA1_EAP = colMeans(BETA1)
a2_opt_hat = ifelse(cbind(1, o1, a1, o2)%*%
                      BETA2_EAP[treat_cols2]>=0, 
                    1,-1)
a1_opt_hat = ifelse(cbind(1, o1)%*%
                      BETA1_EAP[treat_cols1]>=0, 
                    1,-1)
#true optimal regime for stage 1 
true_d1_opt = ifelse(cbind(1, o1) %*% beta1[3:4] >= 0, 1, -1)
true_d2_opt = ifelse(cbind(1,o1, a1, o2) %*% beta2[6:9] >=0, 1,-1) 
mean(a2_opt_hat == true_d2_opt)
[1] 1
mean(a1_opt_hat == true_d1_opt)
[1] 1
# probability of optimal treatments for individual i
i = 30
input2_i = X2[i,treat_cols2]/X2[i,'a2'] #input
input1_i = X1[i,treat_cols1]/X1[i,'a1']
treat2_i = BETA2[,treat_cols2]%*%input2_i 
treat1_i = BETA1[,treat_cols1]%*%input1_i 
(prob_one_is_better_for_a2 = mean(treat2_i>=0))
